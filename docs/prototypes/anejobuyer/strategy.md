# Añejo Buyer Prototype Strategy

## Primary audience

Busy, health-conscious adults in Palm Beach County who want excellent fresh food without shopping, cooking, macro math, or repetitive frozen meal prep.

## Primary user problem

The visitor wants to eat well today and stay consistent, but daily work, errands, fitness, and planning make cooking feel like another job.

## Primary promise

Añejo cooks flavorful real food fresh today and delivers it daily, with standard bowls for simple ordering and macro-personalized portions when the customer wants meals built around a goal.

## Primary action

Order Today.

## Secondary action

See Today's Menu. Macro Portal entry is secondary but visible as a parallel path.

## Messaging hierarchy

1. Fresh food cooked today and delivered today.
2. Bold Cuban-American flavor and real ingredients.
3. Convenience for busy days.
4. Personalization around preferences and goals.
5. Macro-targeted meals when needed.
6. Warm, flexible service through preferences, updates, skip/pause controls, and plain confirmation.
7. Broader offerings such as catering, wholesale, trainer partnerships, and Añejo Hub only after the immediate consumer offer is clear.

## Role of the Macro Portal

The Macro Portal should be introduced as a premium personalization path, not as the homepage's main ask. It explains that the customer enters goal and body inputs, receives calorie/macro/portion guidance, and then orders portion-sized bowls through the existing plan/subscription path.

## Standard and macro ordering coexistence

- Standard path: "I want fresh meals." Customer chooses bowls and delivery details.
- Personalized path: "I want meals built around my goals." Customer starts with Macro Portal inputs and receives portion-sized plan recommendations.
- Both paths use Añejo's same food system and delivery operations.

## Care without sentimental copy

Care should appear through remembered preferences, substitution controls, clear delivery updates, useful SMS/email language, transparent fees, friendly error states, and easy skip/pause controls.

## Premium without corporate

Use the existing cream/black/green/gold brand language, real bowl photos, restrained editorial typography, direct food copy, and operational clarity. Avoid luxury cliches and fake exclusivity.

## Daily fresh delivery versus frozen weekly meal prep

The first viewport should say cooked today and delivered today. Supporting states should show kitchen packout, delivery windows, route progress, and same-day order context. Use "never frozen" only as a concrete proof point, not as a generic badge repeated everywhere.

## Reducing the price objection

Show prices early and explain what is included: cooking, ingredients, packaging, delivery logistics, preferences, personalization, reduced grocery waste, and saved time. Do not hide delivery fee, order minimum, or tax estimates.

## Creating urgency without fake scarcity

Use operational timing: today's menu, same-day prep, lunch/dinner windows, and sold-out states only when tied to real availability. Do not add countdown timers or invented low-stock claims.

## Supporting immediate orders today

Make "Order Today" the primary CTA, show today's menu in the first page flow, include a sticky mobile order CTA, and provide a checkout-entry state that reassures customers what happens before secure payment.
